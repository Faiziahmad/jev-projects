Methodology

How an
engagement runs.

Rules of engagement signed before a single packet. Everything logged, everything reproducible, nothing left behind.

01
Phase one

Recon

Most of the win happens here. I map what you actually expose: subdomains, forgotten staging boxes, leaked creds in old repos, who in the org chart has admin rights. Passive first, then loud only where scope allows.

# output: attack surface map, ranked targets
02
Phase two

Exploitation

Manual, deliberate, in scope. Scanners get a vote but not a veto. I chase the auth logic, the IDOR the fuzzer missed, the misconfigured service account. Anything that could hurt production gets flagged to you before I touch it.

# output: validated foothold, evidence captured
03
Phase three

Post-Exploitation

One shell is a finding; a path to domain admin is a story. I trace how far an attacker gets from that foothold through AD trust, credential reuse and lateral movement, and note every point your SOC should have seen me.

# output: attack path, detection gaps, blast radius
04
Phase four

Reporting

A two-page executive summary your board will read, and a technical section your engineers can act on: repro steps, evidence, CVSS, and the fix that breaks the most chains for the least effort. Then a debrief call and a free retest.

# output: report, debrief, retest, cleanup confirmed
